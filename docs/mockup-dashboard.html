Transcript

Okay so I want to talk through the onboarding flow today because I think we're overcomplicating it. The current five-step wizard feels heavy — users are clicking through it without reading anything.

The problem is we're trying to teach the whole product upfront. What if we just asked for the one thing we actually need, which is the output folder, and then let people discover everything else through use?

I looked at some research on progressive disclosure yesterday and there's a strong argument for deferring configuration until the user actually needs it. The testing we did last month backed this up — people who skipped onboarding entirely performed almost as well as people who completed it.

— 4 min 23 sec —

The other thing I want to flag is the back navigation. Right now if you go back on step three you lose what you entered on step four. That's a bug but it's also a signal that the step model itself is wrong — steps imply irreversible progression and our flow isn't actually like that.

So my proposal: single screen, two required fields — name and output folder — everything else collapsed behind a "More options" disclosure. Ship that and see how far we get.
